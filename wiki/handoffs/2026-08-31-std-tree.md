# Std.Tree — issue #156

## Contract

- `Tree[T]` is one value and a sequence of trees beneath it; `Forest[T]` names a sequence of them.
- A node with no children is a node whose sequence is empty, not a separate case.
- Children keep the order they were given, at every depth and in every traversal.
- `height` of a leaf is 1 and `size` counts the root, so the two agree about what a node is.
- A path is the child index taken at each step from the root; the root's path is empty.
- `prune` removes a rejected node with its subtree; a rejected root answers `None`.
- No partial operation and no sentinel; anything that may have no answer answers `Option`.

## What the language does not allow, and what was done instead

`Functor`, `Applicative`, `Monad`, `Foldable` and `Traversable` cannot be written for this type.
Each needs a type parameter standing for a type constructor, and [[grammar/pudu]] records that v1
has no higher-kinded types. `map`, `fold` and `mapResult` are therefore named on this type, as they
are on every other collection, and no facade pretends otherwise.

Two further consequences, both recorded on the module rather than worked around:

- An effectful traversal cannot be written once for every carrier. `mapResult` names `Result`
  because naming the carrier is exactly what needs the missing feature.
- The language is strict, so an unbounded `unfold` does not finish. `unfoldTo` bounds it by depth,
  and the documentation says which is which rather than implying a tree can be infinite.

Everything else the shape is usually asked for is expressible and is present: the general fold, the
three orders, levels, paths, pruning, and growing from seeds — verified rather than assumed.
